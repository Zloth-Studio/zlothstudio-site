Privacy Policy: Immigration Packet Scanner

Last updated: October 26, 2026

The short version

  • There is no account and no sign-in. Your scans and packet lists stay on your phone.
  • The app doesn't upload your documents. They leave your phone only when you export or share them yourself.
  • Usage data and crash reports are on by default. You can turn them off in Settings > "Share usage data".
  • The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. You choose whether ad partners may use your phone's advertising ID. Premium has no ads.
  • Payments go through Apple. We never see your card or Apple ID details.
  • We don't sell your personal information for money.

This app is not affiliated with USCIS or any government agency.

Who we are

Immigration Packet Scanner is made by Mark Hanna, an individual developer, shown as the seller on its App Store page. We have no servers and no user accounts. To contact us, write to support@zlothstudio.com.

Your documents

Your scans, page images and packet lists (which form you chose, which heading you picked, and the state of each line) are stored only on your phone. The app doesn't upload them. They are not in analytics, crash reports or ad requests.

On iPhone, iOS file protection covers these files, and they are excluded from iCloud and computer backups. This protection comes from iOS, not from encryption we added.

The only way a document leaves your phone is when you export or share it yourself through the share sheet. After that, the app or person you choose handles it under their own terms.

The camera and the photo picker are used only to add pages to a packet.

You can remove your documents at any time: delete the app, or use "Delete all documents" in Settings.

Usage analytics

We use PostHog (US cloud) to see how the app is used. It is on by default. You can turn it off in Settings > "Share usage data".

What is sent: which screens and actions happen, as fixed event names with fixed values and counts (for example, pages scanned, colour mode, export type, language, whether you allowed ads consent or tracking, whether an ad was shown, and whether you started a trial or bought Premium). Also the app version, the type of device and operating system, your language, and a random identifier created by the app. The identifier is not your name, email, phone number or advertising ID.

The app doesn't send document content, labels, file names, or the heading or category you picked. Session replay and touch capture are off.

PostHog receives the IP address of your connection, as any internet service does, and may use it to estimate an approximate location such as your country.

Analytics events may include which of the app's forms a packet is for (I-130, I-485 or I-765), but never the category or heading inside it. Because a form type can hint at an immigration process, it is sent only with the random app identifier. It is not sent with your name, contact details or advertising ID. Turning off "Share usage data" stops it. It is not sent to ad partners, and we don't use it to infer anything about you.

When the app shows a note about missing pages or about front and back, it sends only that a note was shown, which kind it was and a count. It never sends the page numbers, the text printed on a page, or the scan. Page numbers are read on your iPhone and stay there.

The app reads the first page of a scan on your phone to look for a form number (I-130, I-485 or I-765). The page and its text never leave your phone; only the form number may be included in analytics, as described above.

Crash reports

We use Sentry to find and fix crashes. A report contains error details (what went wrong in the code), the app version, the type of device and operating system, and a random identifier. It doesn't contain screenshots or document content. The same "Share usage data" switch turns crash reports off.

Ads

Free users see ads from Google AdMob. Premium subscribers see no ads, and the app makes no ad requests for them.

Before any ad, Google's consent form is shown where Google's consent rules apply (for example in the EEA and UK, and in some US states). On iPhone, Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt asks whether ad partners may use your device's advertising identifier.

  • If you allow, ads may be personalised.
  • If you decline, the advertising identifier is not available to the app or to ad partners, and ads may be less relevant.

You can change your tracking choice at any time in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.

Google receives device and ad-request information and your IP address (which can show your approximate location), so it learns that this device uses this app. Where it is required, Settings > "Ad privacy" reopens Google's choices.

Ads appear only around exporting or re-sharing a packet, not on document or checklist screens.

Subscriptions

Purchases go through Apple. RevenueCat receives your App Store purchase details, basic device information and your IP address, linked to an anonymous ID it creates, so the app knows whether you have Premium and can restore it. We never see your payment card or Apple ID details.

App updates

To download app updates, the app contacts Expo's update service (EAS Update). It receives technical information (app version and platform) and your IP address.

Links to other sites

The app links to uscis.gov and other sites. Their own privacy policies apply once you open them.

Who receives data

The only companies that receive data from the app are the ones named in this policy: PostHog, Sentry, Google (AdMob), RevenueCat, Apple and Expo. Each receives only the data described in its section, and its terms with us require it to protect that data at least as well as this policy does. Google also handles ad data under its own privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Why we may use your data (legal bases)

For people covered by the GDPR or UK GDPR:

  • Analytics and crash reports rest on our legitimate interest in understanding how the app is used and fixing crashes. You can object at any time with the "Share usage data" switch.
  • Ads that are not personalised, and app updates, rest on our legitimate interest in offering a free app that works.
  • Personalised ads rest on your consent, which you can withdraw as described under Ads.
  • Purchases rest on contract.

Our providers may process data in the United States. Where the law requires it, those transfers rely on the safeguards in each provider's terms.

How long data is kept

Documents stay on your phone until you delete them or the app. Each provider keeps analytics and crash data for the period set in its service, then deletes it. We keep no separate copy, because we have no servers.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, delete, correct and port your data, to object to its use, to withdraw consent, and to complain to a data-protection authority.

In the US (California under CCPA/CPRA, and similar state laws), you may have the right to know, delete and correct your data, to opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising, to limit the use of sensitive information, and not to be treated worse for using these rights.

We hold no account, so we usually cannot tell which usage or crash data is yours from your name or email. "Delete all documents" in Settings also replaces the app's random identifier with a new one, so data sent after that is not tied to the old one. Data already sent stays with each provider until its retention period ends. To ask about data already sent, or to ask us to delete it, write to privacy@zlothstudio.com. We will tell you what we can find and delete, and we answer within the time the law requires.

We don't sell your data

We don't sell personal information for money. Under California law, letting ad partners use data for personalised ads may count as "sharing". To opt out, decline tracking when asked, use Settings > "Ad privacy" where it is shown, or change your device's ad settings.

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13, or under the higher age that applies where you live. We don't knowingly collect their data.

Changes

When we change this policy, we update this page and the "Last updated" date.
